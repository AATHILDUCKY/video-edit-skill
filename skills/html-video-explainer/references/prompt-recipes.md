# Adaptable motion-design briefs

Choose a suitable recipe from the content, then rewrite it around that topic's actual mechanism, brand, audience and assets. These are prompts to adapt, not four videos to build whenever the skill is invoked. The motion-film build guide supplies the strict technical pipeline; the normal editor remains the right choice for customizable camera reels.

## Product UI showreel · 30 seconds

> Create a 30-second product showreel for [product/site]. Research its purpose, audience, real interface and brand first. Capture 3–6 real screens and obtain the real logo/type/colors when available; ask only for essential inaccessible material. Keep an evidence/source ledger and invent no screens, features, numbers or proof.
>
> Name one recurring object from the product before the storyboard. Make every scene a transformation of it, from an interrupted 3–5 word problem hook through three or four verified product moments to the real logo/name and one-line CTA. Rebuild the observed UI as independently animatable vector/DOM layers. A visible cursor drives faithful clicks/drags. Measure anchors at runtime after font/layout load.
>
> Use the brand typeface, one principal accent on the recurring object/key word, generous space, a consistent camera push, controlled small-overshoot springs and masks. Match camera state across scenes. Inspect the supplied reference video if one exists. At 120 BPM plan all 60 beats before building; something meaningful changes every beat while copy stays readable.
>
> Build one self-contained 1920×1080 HTML with deterministic `window.seek(t)`, an original offline code score/WAV unless a licensed track is supplied, and 60fps MP4 with three temporal samples. Inspect one frame per beat before full render. Show findings, recurring object and beat map, then build without an extra approval request unless essential input is missing.

Use only genuine UI states. Unavailable product access is a source gap to resolve, not a reason to fabricate a convincing dashboard.

## Connected technique film · 15 seconds

> Choose and name one concept in one line, for example a mark becoming type, structure and a usable interface. Every shot grows out of the previous shot's geometry or typography; no disconnected cuts or crossfades. Use one typeface, at most three deliberate colors plus black/white, and one camera language.
>
> Show five linked three-second movements: kinetic type/hook 0–3s, shape transformation 3–6s, a 2D-to-dimensional moment 6–9s, a meaningful UI/data relationship 9–12s, and synthesis/end title 12–15s. The strongest integrated moment occupies 12–14s; use 14–15s for the title/loop return. At 120 BPM these are six-beat groups, not whole bars. If whole bars are preferred, use the alternative allocation in the direction guide.
>
> Use analytical springs/exponential easing, velocity-aware temporal blur, measured match anchors, separate text handoff windows, and a final frame that returns to the actual opening composition. Compose the offline score with every-beat kick, scene hits, and a riser into the strongest moment. Show the concept and 30-beat storyboard, then build the single HTML, WAV and 60fps three-sample MP4 after inspecting beat frames.

A data/UI moment must demonstrate a coherent relationship; do not invent user-growth claims just to have a chart.

## Business explainer · 30 seconds

> Explain [what the business sells] to [who it serves], using [verified name/brand/colors]. Build five six-second scenes: the customer's problem, the offer, how it works in three steps, a genuine proof point, and the brand/CTA. Get missing essential business facts first and never make up testimonials, numbers or customer counts.
>
> Choose a recurring object from the real customer task or offer. Use bold brand type, concise copy, masks and connected transformations. At 120 BPM each scene has 12 beats. Introduce the three steps on successive beats, then use the remaining beats to reveal their relationship while the labels remain readable. Let mechanism, cursor or camera changes carry the beat instead of flashing unreadable text.
>
> Show the five copy blocks and beat map, then build one 1920×1080 deterministic HTML, offline score/WAV and 60fps three-temporal-sample MP4. Inspect beat frames and verify proof against its source. Keep the brand ending clear. If proof has not been supplied or verified, ask for it or explicitly propose a demonstrated process/result in its place rather than pretending to have evidence.

## Graphic dot / spring loop · about 15 seconds

> Use [toggle label], [hero word], [spring overshoot parameter], [final card], [accent] and [licensed track or composed score]. Gather required custom inputs in one concise group; use disclosed defaults for optional skipped inputs. Keep the chosen warm off-white, near-black and orange/brand treatment consistent.
>
> One dot connects the film: click-driven toggle/type 0–2s, shared-function dot-and-spring graph 2–5s, the same dot growing into circle/square/pill states 5–8s, circular kinetic type/intentional halftone geometry 8–11s, end card 11–13s, and a connected return to the exact opening 13–15s. Attach the dot to the measured hero glyph until its jump. The graph and dot must use the same closed-form spring; a percentage must match that designed response.
>
> Plan all 30 beats and show four stills—toggle, graph, type, end card—before the full render when requested. Continue after those previews unless the user asked for an approval pause. Compute every frame through `seek(t)`, preserve shape identity and velocity, fill past all corners before a wipe, and match the first/last camera, cursor, dot and typography. Render one frame per beat for inspection before the HTML/WAV/60fps three-sample MP4.

## Shared art-direction filter

For these connected motion briefs: no particle bursts, glow, lens flare, camera shake, RGB split, generic gradients, bouncy easing, arbitrary transitions or crossfades. Keep changes motivated by an existing element. A deliberate halftone grid is allowed when it is the actual concept; it is not a random-particle effect. Avoid visual dead time, but retain legibility—an object/line/camera can change on the beat while a label stays long enough to read. Always adapt the composition rather than presenting an unchanged starter as an original film.
